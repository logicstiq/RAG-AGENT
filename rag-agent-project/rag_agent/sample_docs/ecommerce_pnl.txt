P&L Economics for a Managed E-commerce Brand

A brand's e-commerce P&L has more line items than a simple
revenue-minus-cost view, and most of the "where did the margin go"
confusion in this business comes from one or two of these lines being
tracked inconsistently or omitted entirely from a quick analysis.

Starting from the top: Gross Revenue is total sales before any
deductions. Net Revenue subtracts returns and promotional discounts.
From Net Revenue, Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) -- unit cost, inbound
freight, duties if imported -- is subtracted to get Gross Profit.

Below gross profit, marketplace-specific costs apply: the referral fee
(Amazon's cut, category-dependent, commonly 8-17% of the sale price),
FBA fulfillment fees (pick/pack plus weight-based shipping, which scale
with dimensional weight, not just actual weight), and storage fees
(monthly, with a steep multiplier during Q4 peak season and additional
long-term storage surcharges for slow-moving inventory sitting beyond
roughly six months). Advertising spend (Sponsored Products, Sponsored
Brands, DSP) is usually tracked as a percentage of sales, called ACoS
(Advertising Cost of Sale) at the campaign level or TACoS (Total
Advertising Cost of Sale, including organic sales in the denominator) at
the account level.

Contribution Margin is what remains after COGS, marketplace fees, and
advertising -- this is the number that actually reflects whether a SKU
is profitable to keep selling, and it is the figure most relevant to
reorder and discontinuation decisions, as distinct from Gross Margin,
which ignores advertising and fulfillment entirely and can make a losing
SKU look healthy.

Below contribution margin, the management company's own fee (the
revenue-share or retainer from the brand contract) is subtracted to
reach the brand's Net Profit for that channel. If the management company
itself is reporting on its own P&L for the engagement, its revenue is
that same management fee, and its own costs are labor, software
tooling, and any ad spend it fronts before billing back to the brand.

The most common analytical mistake in this P&L is evaluating SKU
profitability on Gross Margin alone: a SKU with a 60% gross margin can
still be a net loser once a 15% referral fee, FBA fees, and a 25% ACoS
are layered in, which is why contribution margin, not gross margin, is
the number that should drive reorder, pricing, and delisting decisions.
