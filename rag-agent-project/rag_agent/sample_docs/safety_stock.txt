Safety Stock and Reorder Points

Safety stock is the buffer inventory a company holds beyond expected demand
to protect against uncertainty in demand forecasts and supplier lead times.
Without safety stock, any spike in demand or delay in replenishment can
trigger a stockout, which in e-commerce usually means lost sales, a Buy Box
penalty, and in marketplace contexts a hit to account health metrics.

A common way to calculate safety stock is:
Safety Stock = Z-score x Standard Deviation of Demand x sqrt(Lead Time)

The Z-score corresponds to the desired service level. A 95% service level
uses a Z-score of 1.65, while a 99% service level uses roughly 2.33. Higher
service levels require disproportionately more safety stock, which is why
most operations teams tier their service-level targets by SKU velocity and
margin rather than using one blanket number.

The reorder point is the inventory level at which a new purchase order
should be triggered, and is typically:
Reorder Point = (Average Daily Demand x Lead Time in Days) + Safety Stock

Lead time variability matters as much as demand variability. A supplier
with a stable 30-day lead time but wildly inconsistent demand needs less
safety stock than a supplier whose lead time swings between 20 and 60 days
even with steady demand, because reorder point calculations are only as
good as the lead time assumption feeding them.
