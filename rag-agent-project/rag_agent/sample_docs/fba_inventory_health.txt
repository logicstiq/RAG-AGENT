Amazon FBA Inventory Performance Basics

The Inventory Performance Index (IPI) score is Amazon's composite measure
of how efficiently a seller manages FBA inventory. It factors in excess
inventory, sell-through rate, stranded inventory, and in-stock rate. A low
IPI score can trigger storage limits, which directly constrains how much
new inventory a seller can send in, making replenishment planning and
safety stock calculations even more important when the score is borderline.

Excess inventory is stock that has been sitting in a fulfillment center
without selling at a healthy pace relative to its size and sales history.
Amazon flags it because it increases long-term storage fees and ties up
warehouse capacity. The fix is almost always a demand-side one: either
discount the SKU to move it, create a removal order, or adjust future
purchase order quantities using a tighter reorder point calculation.

Stranded inventory refers to units physically in a fulfillment center that
aren't appearing as available for sale, usually due to a listing error,
missing content, or a suppressed detail page. Unlike excess inventory,
stranded inventory is a catalog/listing problem, not a demand-planning
problem, and needs to be fixed in Seller Central's listing quality tools
rather than through purchase order adjustments.

In-stock rate measures the percentage of time a SKU had available
sellable inventory. It is one of the clearest downstream signals of whether
a seller's reorder points and safety stock levels are actually working,
since a low in-stock rate usually traces back to either an underestimated
reorder point or a lead-time assumption that no longer matches reality.
