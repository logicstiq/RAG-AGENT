Incoterms for E-commerce Sellers

Incoterms define who is responsible for shipping costs, insurance, customs
clearance, and risk of loss at each stage of an international shipment.
They matter enormously for landed cost calculations, which in turn feed
directly into margin analysis and pricing decisions for any SKU sourced
overseas.

FOB (Free On Board) means the seller is responsible for costs and risk up
to loading the goods onto the shipping vessel at the port of origin. The
buyer takes over risk and cost from that point, including ocean freight,
insurance, and import duties. FOB is the most common term used by Amazon
and other marketplace sellers importing from manufacturers in Asia, because
it gives the buyer control over freight forwarder selection and shipping
cost, which matters a lot when planning inventory replenishment timing.

DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) shifts nearly all responsibility to the seller,
who must handle shipping, insurance, customs clearance, and duty payment
all the way to the buyer's specified location. This is simpler for the
buyer but typically more expensive overall, since the seller builds in a
margin on the logistics and duty handling.

EXW (Ex Works) places minimum responsibility on the seller, who only needs
to make the goods available at their own premises. The buyer handles
everything else, including export clearance, which can be a problem for
buyers unfamiliar with the origin country's export regulations.

Choosing the wrong Incoterm is one of the most common landed-cost modeling
mistakes in new import programs, because it's easy to forget which freight,
insurance, and duty line items are already included in a quoted unit price.
