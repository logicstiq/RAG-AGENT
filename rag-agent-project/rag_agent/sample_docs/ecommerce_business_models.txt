E-commerce Business Models: 1P, 3P, and DTC

Most brands selling on Amazon and other marketplaces operate through one or
more of three distinct commercial models, and the choice has downstream
consequences for pricing control, cash flow, inventory ownership, and who
actually manages the catalog.

1P (First Party / Vendor Central): The brand sells inventory wholesale
directly to the retailer (Amazon Retail), which then owns the inventory,
sets the retail price, and controls the buy box. The brand gets a purchase
order and gets paid on vendor terms, typically net-30 to net-90, often with
chargebacks for compliance issues (late shipments, mislabeled cartons,
missing ASNs). Margins are usually tighter here because the retailer takes
the retail margin, but demand is more predictable since it is driven by PO
volume rather than real-time consumer behavior.

3P (Third Party / Seller Central / Marketplace): The brand (or its
authorized seller) lists and sells directly to the end consumer through the
marketplace, retaining ownership of inventory until the sale, setting its
own price, and controlling its own content and promotions. Amazon takes a
referral fee (category-dependent, commonly 8-17%) plus fulfillment fees if
using FBA. This model gives the brand far more control over brand
presentation, pricing, and promotional cadence, but also full responsibility
for demand forecasting, inventory risk, and account health metrics like
Order Defect Rate and Inventory Performance Index.

DTC (Direct-to-Consumer): The brand sells through its own site (commonly
Shopify, WooCommerce, or a custom storefront), owning the full customer
relationship, the data, and the margin, but also bearing full responsibility
for driving traffic, conversion, fulfillment, and customer service. DTC
margins are structurally the highest of the three models because there is no
marketplace referral fee, but customer acquisition cost (CAC) replaces that
fee as the dominant cost line, and fulfillment economics are usually worse
at low volume than they are for an Amazon-fulfilled 3P listing.

Most brands with any meaningful scale run a hybrid: 1P for baseline
wholesale volume and retail distribution, 3P for marketplace growth and
promotional flexibility, and DTC for margin, brand control, and first-party
customer data. A brand management company's core value proposition is
usually optimizing the mix and the operational execution across all three
simultaneously, since each channel has a different P&L shape, a different
inventory pool (or a shared one that needs careful allocation), and
different compliance requirements.
