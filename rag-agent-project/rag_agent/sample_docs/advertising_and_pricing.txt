Advertising and Pricing Strategy

Advertising and pricing are run as one connected system in marketplace
e-commerce, not two separate functions, because a price change shifts ad
efficiency and an ad budget change shifts the price a product can
profitably sell at.

Sponsored Products is the baseline ad type on Amazon: keyword- or
product-targeted ads that show in search results and on competitor detail
pages, charged on a cost-per-click (CPC) basis through an auction. Sponsored
Brands adds a branded headline and multiple products in a single ad unit,
typically used higher in the funnel to build awareness rather than drive
immediate conversion. DSP (Demand-Side Platform) runs programmatic display
and video ads both on and off Amazon, usually reserved for larger budgets
aimed at retargeting or upper-funnel reach rather than direct-response
conversion.

ACoS (Advertising Cost of Sale) measures ad spend as a percentage of the
sales that specific ad generated -- a campaign-level efficiency metric.
TACoS (Total Advertising Cost of Sale) instead divides total ad spend by
total sales, organic included, which is the more honest long-term metric
because a campaign can have a terrible ACoS while still profitably pulling
organic sales upward through improved ranking. Chasing a low ACoS on every
individual campaign can actually suppress overall growth if it means
turning off campaigns that were driving organic lift the ACoS number
doesn't capture.

Bid management follows a standard cycle: start with broad or automatic
targeting to discover which search terms actually convert, harvest the
converting terms into their own tightly-controlled exact-match campaigns,
and negative-match the terms that spent money without converting. This
harvesting cycle typically runs on a 1-2 week cadence for an active
account; running it less frequently lets budget leak into non-converting
terms for longer than necessary.

Pricing strategy on a marketplace is rarely static. A common approach,
especially for a new listing or a new market entry, is to launch at a
deliberately high price relative to competitors, then test price
reductions on a defined cadence against conversion rate and margin impact,
with guardrails on how far price can move before requiring approval --
the same pricing architecture used in new-market brand onboarding.
Lightning Deals and coupons are short-duration promotional price cuts that
trade margin for a visibility boost (a "deal" badge, placement in deal
pages) and are usually budgeted as a distinct line separate from everyday
pricing, since the sales lift during a deal period is not a reliable
predictor of post-deal baseline velocity.

The dependency worth remembering: raising price without adjusting ad bids
can make a previously-profitable keyword unprofitable overnight, because
the same CPC now represents a smaller fraction of a now-larger margin
cushion -- or the inverse, where a price cut makes a keyword suddenly
worth bidding much more aggressively on. Pricing and advertising decisions
made by two different people without a shared view of contribution margin
are a common source of silently eroding profitability that doesn't show up
until a monthly P&L review.
